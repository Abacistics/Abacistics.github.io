Rapid Braking Fun Facts

Fact #1

The First Automotive Brake

Louis Renault invented the drum brake design in 1902, using wrapped internal shoes that pressed outward against a metal drum to stop effectively!

Fact #2

Extreme Heat & ERS

High-performance race car brakes reach over 1,000°C (1,800°F) during sudden stops. Modern hybrid cars capture this kinetic energy through Energy Recovery Systems (ERS)!

Fact #3

Nature's Braking Champion

Cheetahs can decelerate by up to 9 miles per hour in a single stride using specialized non-retractable claws and broad foot pads for incredible braking traction!

Upcoming Competition

One Giant Leap

Submissions open on September 1st! The new live competition leaderboard will go live at the beginning of September.

Overview

In honor of the first moon landing, students are tasked with designing a launching mechanism and soft-landing system for an action figure of their choice. To ensure a safe landing for all toys, students will be judged on their ability to keep the figure airborne for as long as possible.

Rules & Safety

  • Scoring: The competition is based strictly on airtime.
  • Sizing: All submissions must fit into a 24x24x24 inch cube (approx. 61×61×61 cm).
  • Verification: Submissions are timed by participants and subject to review by the Abacistics admin team.
  • Materials: All building materials are valid (wood, metal, plastic, Lego bricks, etc.).
  • Budget Limit: All projects must cost less than $100. This rule will only be used with administrative discretion.
  • Safety Constraint: No chemical reactions may be used in launching mechanisms. Think creatively, but safely!

Submission Process

To complete an official entry, students must submit:

  1. Division selection (Lower or Upper Elementary).
  2. Student and parent/guardian contact info and country.
  3. An unlisted YouTube video link showcasing the official launch and airtime result.
  4. Display consent signed by parent/guardian.

Reminder for Parents

Parents are encouraged to assist, but not take over student projects. Assisting in drilling, using hot glue, and filming are all acceptable forms of assistance.

General Rule:

"Ask your kid if they think it's cheating. They are generally pretty good at knowing."

If you are unsure if an action violates this guideline, email us at Abacistics.admin@gmail.com.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What size or weight should the action figure be? v

Any standard action figure or minifigure works! We recommend figures between 2 to 6 inches tall so they can easily attach to your launcher or parachute rig.

How do I accurately measure and record airtime? v

Airtime is measured from the exact moment the figure leaves the launch mechanism until it touches the ground. Use a phone stopwatch while recording your video proof!

How long does it take for a submission to appear on the leaderboard? v

The Abacistics admin team reviews all incoming entries for video verification within 24–48 hours before updating the official rankings.

Can I submit more than one attempt if I improve my project? v

Yes! You can re-submit the form with a new video link at any point before the challenge deadline to update your score on the leaderboard.

About Abacistics

We are a group of high school and college students dedicated to making hands-on engineering accessible, engaging, and welcoming for younger students.

The founders of Abacistics are active competitors in various high-level robotics and engineering challenges—including FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC), VEXU, V5RC, and Formula SAE (FSAE). Having navigated these complex programs ourselves, we understand firsthand the immense rewards and the intimidation factor that comes with entering the world of engineering.

We created Abacistics to bridge that gap. By offering focused, friendly challenges, we aim to inspire the next generation of builders, problem solvers, and innovators early on. We have all had transformative experiences through engineering, and we hope that through Abacistics, you will too.
